<h2 id="three-myths">Three Myths That Keep SaaS Teams Stuck on AWS Marketplace:</h2>

<h3 id="myth-1">Myth #1: "List it and they'll come"</h3>
<p>High-value B2B deals don't happen through public listings on AWS Marketplace. They happen through Private Offers, custom deals with negotiated pricing built for a specific buyer.</p>
<p>Your listing isn't a lead gen machine. It's a procurement vehicle.</p>
<p>If you're waiting for inbound, you're waiting forever.</p>

<h3 id="myth-2">Myth #2: "Our sales team will figure it out"</h3>
<p>They won't.</p>
<p>Selling through AWS Marketplace requires a different motion. Your reps need to know how to register deals in AWS Partner Central, how to articulate why burning cloud commit matters to a CIO, and how to create Private Offers without losing three days to back-and-forth.</p>
<p>Without a playbook, they'll revert to what they know. Which means they'll ignore the Marketplace entirely.</p>

<h3 id="myth-3">Myth #3: "We'll handle operations later"</h3>
<p>Later never comes.</p>
<p>Manual offer creation feels fine when you're doing two deals a quarter. At ten deals a month, it becomes a bottleneck that kills velocity.</p>
<p>No integrated system means no scale. Operations aren't the cleanup crew. They're the engine.</p>

<h2 id="where-it-breaks">Where It Actually Breaks:</h2>

<p>I've seen this pattern repeat across dozens of ISVs. The failure happens in four places.</p>

<h3 id="break-1"> 1. Readiness Failure: The Shallow Checklist</h3>
<p>Most teams treat readiness like a form to fill out. Tax info, banking details, product page live. Done.</p>
<p>Real readiness means mapping your sales workflows to AWS-native processes. It means getting through the Foundational Technical Review (FTR) so AWS field teams actually trust your product enough to recommend it.</p>
<p>Without FTR, you're invisible to co-sell. With it, you're tagged "Reviewed by AWS", the credibility signal that moves deals forward.</p>

<h3 id="break-2"> 2. Listing Failure: The Brochure No One Reads</h3>
<p>Your product detail page gets treated like a placeholder. Generic copy. No customer proof. No clear connection to how you help AWS customers burn commits faster.</p>
<p>AWS sellers search the Marketplace when they need a solution for an active deal. If your page doesn't immediately signal value and trust, they move on.</p>
<p>A strong listing answers two questions in ten seconds: "What does this do for my customer?" and "Why should I bet my credibility on it?"</p>

<h3 id="break-3"> 3. Seller Motion Failure: The One-Off Deal</h3>
<p>Your team treats every AWS Marketplace deal like a science experiment. They don't register opportunities in Partner Central through APN Customer Engagements (ACE). They can't explain how the Marketplace accelerates procurement. Private Offers take a week to create because no one remembers the last time they did one.</p>
<p>This isn't a sales problem. It's a system problem.</p>
<p>Repeatable wins come from repeatable process. You need ACE hygiene, standardized offer workflows, and a narrative that makes AWS field sellers want to help you close.</p>

<h3 id="break-4"> 4. Operations Failure: The Spreadsheet Spiral</h3>
<p>Deal volume grows. Your backend doesn't.</p>
<p>Offers get tracked in Slack threads. Renewals live in someone's calendar. Finance can't reconcile disbursements because there's no source of truth connecting the CRM to what AWS reports.</p>
<p>Velocity dies in the manual work between systems.</p>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions GTM leaders ask:</h2>

<p><strong>1. What is the difference between listing and selling on AWS Marketplace?</strong><br>
Listing is technical access. Selling requires active co-sell, Private Offers, and operational discipline.</p>

<p><strong>2. How does AWS co-sell work in reality?</strong><br>
Deals are registered in ACE inside Partner Central. AWS AMs reviews them and provide co-sell support through introductions or procurement acceleration.</p>

<p><strong>3. Why are Private Offers critical?</strong><br>
Because most enterprise deals on AWS Marketplace use them. Public pricing rarely closes large contracts.</p>

<p><strong>4. Is the Foundational Technical Review required?</strong><br>
No. But without it, many co-sell paths stay closed. It is a trust signal to AWS sellers.</p>

<p><strong>5. How does billing work?</strong><br>
AWS bills the customer and disburses funds monthly. The complexity lies in tracking, not collecting.</p>
